# Scientific Dossier: *Ferrucoccus scuttans*

**Common name:** Ironback Mason  
**Ecosystem role:** Mutualist - Mineral mason

## Profile

A low copper-red disc with overlapping iron plates and mineral-collecting cilia. This cryptid shares the same deep-sea hydrothermal-vent ecosystem as *Hydrosaltator amictus*.

- **Habitat:** iron-rich chimney terraces beneath the host's feeding routes
- **Type:** Mutualist
- **Role:** Mineral mason

## Detailed behavior

The Ironback Mason patrols chimney terraces in short, deliberate shuffles, testing loose grains with its cilia before drawing useful fragments beneath its body. It settles over a crack, presses mineral-rich adhesive into the gap, and adds particles in successive layers. Repair follows quiet intervals in vent flow, giving its cement time to hold before the next disturbance.

The mason favors worn host routes, where repeated footfalls expose fresh damage. Sulfur energy packets from the harvester support long repair bouts. When turbulence rises, it abandons loose cargo and flattens against the rock; when supplies run low, it maintains existing footholds before extending a terrace. Every pebble remains, in its opinion, already reserved.

## Detailed anatomy

**Armored disc.** The low body is covered by overlapping iron-rich plates, with smaller scales around the flexible rim. Their copper-red surfaces are deposited mineral armor over living tissue, rather than a solid metal body. Soft seams between plates permit the disc to bend over uneven stone, and new mineral layers accumulate along the growing edges.

**Collecting fringe.** Rows of mineral-collecting cilia extend from protected slots around the margin. Outer cilia probe and sort particles; shorter inner rows sweep selected grains into grooves leading beneath the disc. Oversized fragments are released before they can jam these channels.

**Binding foot.** The broad underside forms a terrace-binding foot with alternating adhesive patches and shallow collecting recesses. Successive patches attach and release to produce the characteristic scuttle. Gland-like pockets deliver cement to the center of the foot, where pressure shapes a repair against the substrate.

**Interior and maintenance.** Storage compartments hold fine mineral grains separately from nutrient reserves. A flexible internal support layer distributes compression across the body. Damaged plates can be shed individually, leaving the softer surface temporarily sheltered beneath neighboring scales until a replacement deposit hardens.
