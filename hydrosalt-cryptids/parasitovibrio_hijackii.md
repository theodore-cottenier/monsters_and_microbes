# Scientific Dossier: *Parasitovibrio hijackii*

**Common name:** Matrix Jackal  
**Ecosystem role:** Parasite - Matrix-housing thief

## Profile

A dark hooked rod with a sucker collar and a backpack-like brood pouch. This cryptid shares the same deep-sea hydrothermal-vent ecosystem as *Hydrosaltator amictus*.

- **Habitat:** the underside of Gelatinicollum vulcanum sheets
- **Type:** Parasite
- **Role:** Matrix-housing thief

## Detailed behavior

The Matrix Jackal travels along the shaded underside of gel sheets, pausing at thin seams and poorly repaired edges. It attaches its collar, hooks a loose flap, and bends its body to peel the gel away in small increments. The stolen material is gathered around its brood pouch, leaving a ragged channel that may weaken the host's housing.

Host grooming or approaching repair cells interrupt a theft. The jackal holds still beneath its cargo or releases its grip and drifts to another fold. It favors already damaged housing over thick, healthy sheets. As the brood matures, young individuals leave under the cover of discarded gel, and scavengers consume the trails left behind.

## Detailed anatomy

**Hooked body.** The dark rod curves around a reinforced inner edge, giving it leverage against soft matrix sheets. A flexible outer covering permits bending while longitudinal supporting fibers resist stretching. Its shape is rod-like, but the fictional brood-bearing body is more elaborate than a simple bacterial cell.

**Sucker collar.** A broad collar surrounds the attachment end. Its soft rim seals against wet gel while a recessed center provides temporary suction and adhesion. Small sensory pits along the rim detect contact and deformation, helping the parasite distinguish a firm anchor from a flap that will tear away.

**Gel-hook and feeding surface.** The hook projects just beyond the collar and has a hard tip over a flexible base. A groove behind it guides loosened gel toward an absorptive underside. The organism can harvest dissolved nutrients there while reserving intact scraps for shelter.

**Brood pouch.** The dorsal pouch has an expandable outer wall and a narrow, closable opening. Internal folds separate developing offspring from coarse mineral grit. Adhesive ridges on the pouch exterior retain stolen gel, creating the backpack outline. Contractile bands around its neck regulate release when the young are ready to disperse.

## Relationships in the wider food web

During a gel theft, the Matrix Jackal may also draw exposed *Gelatinocella wardii* repair cells into its feeding groove and digest them. This opportunistic predation supplies concentrated food while reducing the repair response around the stolen patch. Its collar can retain a repair cell against the absorptive underside; digestive secretions act there before soluble products are taken up.
