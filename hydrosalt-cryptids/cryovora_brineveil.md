# Scientific Dossier: *Cryovora brineveil*

**Common name:** Cold Veil Drifter

## Profile

A pale blue veil of bead-like cells joined by a delicate thread. This cryptid shares the same deep-sea hydrothermal-vent ecosystem as *Hydrosaltator amictus*.

- **Habitat:** cold brine curtains where vent water meets abyssal seawater

Moves like a drifting handkerchief and refuses to admit currents do most of the work.

## Detailed behavior

The Cold Veil Drifter spreads across a boundary between brine and warmer vent water, allowing each bead to sample slightly different conditions. Unequal tension along its connecting thread makes the veil curl toward a favorable current. It travels mainly by drift, using changes in shape to slow, turn, or avoid being drawn into a hot plume.

Passing Hydrosaltator wakes briefly stretch the colony into a streamer. After the wake subsides, it opens again and releases accumulated trace salts near the matrix margin without consuming the host's food. In turbulent water the beads bunch into a compact chain; damaged sections separate into smaller viable groups instead of pulling the whole veil apart.

## Detailed anatomy

**Bead-cell veil.** The colony consists of rounded, pale blue cells arranged along a branching, flexible support thread. Each bead retains its own membrane and living contents. A clear shared coating bridges neighboring cells, producing the impression of a continuous veil while leaving open spaces through which water can pass.

**Salt vesicles.** Internal membrane-bound pockets isolate concentrated salts from the rest of each cell. Their filling and emptying alter local water balance and bead firmness. Surface transport sites regulate exchange, while nutrient droplets occupy separate compartments so stored salts do not overwhelm the feeding machinery.

**Navigation thread.** The thread combines an elastic core with an adhesive outer coating. Its junctions transmit tension between beads; short sensory projections at those junctions register the direction of drag. This distributed arrangement provides orientation without a head or central navigation organ.

**Colony edges and repair.** Edge beads carry longer extensions that catch weak currents. Narrow connections between groups act as break points under excessive strain. After separation, surviving beads seal the exposed coating and extend new thread, rebuilding a smaller veil around the remaining cells.
