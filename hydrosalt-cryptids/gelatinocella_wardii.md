# Scientific Dossier: *Gelatinocella wardii*

**Common name:** Matrix Gardener  
**Ecosystem role:** Mutualist - Housing stabilizer

## Profile

A cluster of translucent oval cells linked by broad ribbon-like braces. This cryptid shares the same deep-sea hydrothermal-vent ecosystem as *Hydrosaltator amictus*.

- **Habitat:** inside the gelatinous matrix surrounding Hydrosaltator
- **Type:** Mutualist

## Detailed behavior

The Matrix Gardener responds to stretching gel by gathering along a tear's edges. Repair cells attach on both sides, extend ribbon braces across the opening, and deposit fresh binding material between them. The first patch is loose enough to flex with the current; repeated passes thicken it while leaving channels for water and dissolved food.

Sugar deliveries from Saccharoplasma support rapid repair after a host leap or gel theft. In quiet periods, gardeners thin blocked pores and reinforce nursery folds around the host's young. If damage exceeds the available material, they stabilize the remaining edge rather than sealing the whole sheet into an impermeable lump. Mature clusters separate into smaller repair teams as housing expands.

## Detailed anatomy

**Repair cells.** Translucent oval cells form a loose, connected cluster. Each cell has a resilient membrane, a central nuclear compartment, and conspicuous vesicles containing matrix-binding secretions. The outer coating adheres to Gelatinicollum's gel without replacing the larger organism's living sheet.

**Ribbon braces.** Broad extracellular ribbons link cells across gaps. A fibrous inner layer resists pulling, while a softer coating bonds to the existing matrix. Their flat shape spreads force over a wider area than a thin thread, reducing the chance that a fresh patch will cut through its own anchor points.

**Matrix pores.** Open spaces between braces function as exchange pores. Their edges are lined with adjustable gel deposits, allowing the cluster to narrow a large opening or reopen a clogged one. A matrix pore is therefore a feature of the communal patch, rather than a mouth in an individual cell.

**Transport and renewal.** Small nutrient stores lie between the nucleus and secretion vesicles. Contact bridges keep adjacent repair cells aligned as the sheet flexes. Dividing cells remain attached until new braces can support them; old, stiff ribbons are loosened and replaced so the repaired housing retains its elasticity.

## Relationships in the wider food web

The Matrix Jackal can consume exposed repair cells while stripping gel. Surviving gardeners close broken contact bridges and retreat behind intact braces. Leapspore transports dormant gardener recruits to other terraces, allowing damaged patches to be repopulated.
