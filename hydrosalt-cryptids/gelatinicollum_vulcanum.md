# Scientific Dossier: *Gelatinicollum vulcanum*

**Common name:** Matrix Host

**Ecosystem role:** Living housing for the HydroSalts community

## Profile

A larger matrix-forming organism whose hydrated sheets shelter *Hydrosaltator amictus*, the Emberspit harvester, the Sugar-Silk refiner, and their neighbors.

*The expanded behavior and anatomy below are speculative worldbuilding for this fictional organism.*

## Detailed behavior

Gelatinicollum spreads as living sheets over vent terraces, extending lobes into sheltered crevices and withdrawing from violently flushed edges. It incorporates water and secreted gel into new housing, leaving channels where harvesters, refiners, and Hydrosaltator can exchange materials. Growth is fastest where the community returns nutrients and keeps those channels open.

Small tears cause nearby folds to contract and thicken while Gelatinocella adds reinforcing braces. Repeated theft leaves the sheet retracting around surviving anchors, whereas steady nutrient supply encourages new nursery pockets and connecting corridors. Detached living fragments can settle elsewhere and rebuild a smaller matrix. It behaves like a slow habitat-forming colony: its responses unfold as changes in sheet tension, pore size, and growth direction.

## Detailed anatomy

**Living sheet and secreted gel.** The matrix host consists of a thin living network embedded in a much larger volume of translucent hydrated gel. The gel supplies most of the visible bulk, while interconnected cellular strands produce and maintain it. It is a distinct organism from the smaller repair clusters of Gelatinocella wardii.

**Outer skin and basal anchors.** A denser surface film limits erosion and traps useful organic particles. Beneath it, fibrous reinforcement keeps the sheet from flowing apart. Broad adhesive pads attach the underside to mineral terraces, with flexible necks allowing the housing to sway without peeling away from every anchor.

**Channels and chambers.** Interconnected pores carry water and dissolved resources through the housing. Larger folds provide sheltered chambers for the core community; narrower passages separate hot harvester pockets from cooler refinement areas. Their walls are deformable gel supported by living strands, allowing the colony to remodel a passage as vent conditions change.

**Secretory and storage regions.** Swollen nodes along the living network contain secretion vesicles and nutrient reserves. Growing margins have especially active nodes that lay down fresh material, while mature interior strands maintain existing walls. Waste and loose particles leave through open channels instead of a single excretory organ.

**Repair and dispersal margins.** Torn edges contract around surviving strands and acquire a fresh surface film. Living lobes pinched off with sufficient reserves can serve as founding fragments, whereas stolen gel without living tissue provides temporary shelter but cannot independently rebuild the organism.
