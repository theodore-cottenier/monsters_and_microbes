# Scientific Dossier: *Sulfurivora capsida*

**Common name:** Emberspit Phage  
**Ecosystem role:** Virus - Archaea parasite

## Profile

A geometric sulfur-yellow capsid with a filament tail and angular docking fibers. This cryptid shares the same deep-sea hydrothermal-vent ecosystem as *Hydrosaltator amictus*.

- **Habitat:** inside Sulfuribacillus thermophilus harvesters
- **Type:** Virus
- **Role:** Archaea parasite

## Field behavior

Has no metabolism of its own and yet maintains an astonishingly busy schedule.

## Detailed behavior

Free Emberspit Phage particles move with water and matrix fluids until their docking fibers encounter a compatible harvester cell. Attachment brings the tail against the cell surface, allowing the genome to enter. The infected cell then supplies the materials and machinery for assembling new particles; the virus has no independent feeding, swimming, or decision-making stage.

New particles accumulate before the host cell breaks down and releases them into adjacent sulfur channels. Local clusters of susceptible cells permit an outbreak, while isolated pockets and depleted hosts limit its spread. Harvester loss opens space for other community members, although a large outbreak also reduces the energy supply available to the matrix.

## Detailed anatomy

**Capsid shell.** The sulfur-yellow head is a geometric protein shell assembled from repeating subunits. Raised edges reinforce its facets, enclosing a compact genome. The color is part of the fictional illustration and does not imply that the shell is made of elemental sulfur or produces energy from it.

**Genome packing.** The genome thread is densely arranged inside the head and exits through a specialized portal at one vertex. Protective internal contacts hold it during transport. There is no nucleus, cytoplasm, digestive compartment, or store of metabolic fuel inside the particle.

**Tail and docking fibers.** A narrow filamentous tail extends from the portal and ends in an attachment complex. Angular fibers project outward to contact the harvester's surface before the tail tip engages. Flexible fiber bases accommodate an uneven cell coating while preserving the alignment needed for genome transfer.

**Assembly and empty particles.** Head, tail, and fibers are manufactured inside the infected host and joined into complete particles. After genome delivery, the shell remains outside as an empty casing. Its similar outline can make spent particles resemble infectious ones in a purely visual field illustration.
