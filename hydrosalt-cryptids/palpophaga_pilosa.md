# Scientific Dossier: *Palpophaga pilosa*

**Common name:** Moustache Mite  
**Ecosystem role:** Ectoparasite - Palp grazer

## Profile

A tiny flattened amber parasite with comb-like mouthparts and a clamp tail. This cryptid shares the same deep-sea hydrothermal-vent ecosystem as *Hydrosaltator amictus*.

- **Habitat:** the furry head palps of Hydrosaltator amictus
- **Type:** Ectoparasite
- **Role:** Palp grazer

## Field behavior

Insists it is a barber, despite never accepting payment.

## Detailed behavior

The Moustache Mite grips a palp filament with its tail and works forward with short strokes of its comb mouthparts. It gathers grooming oils and loosened mineral particles, then shifts its anchor to reach a fresh strip. A few mites clear accumulated grit; dense groups scrape repeatedly at the same sites and interfere with the host's sensory hairs.

When a grooming sweep approaches, the parasite flattens along the filament and tightens its clamp. Persistent disturbance sends it into a sheltered matrix fold, where it rests and leaves young before returning to the palps. Individuals cluster around oily filament bases, turning a useful cleaning service into an extremely crowded barbershop.

## Detailed anatomy

**Flattened body.** A thin amber dorsal shield covers a soft, shallow body that can fit between the host's hairs. Flexible side margins fold downward to reduce its exposed profile during grooming. The translucent shield reveals darker food stores without providing a clear view of every internal structure.

**Comb mouthparts.** Opposed rows of fine teeth rake material toward a small oral opening. The outer teeth separate grit from the host's filament, while shorter inner teeth retain softer food. Flexible joints let the comb follow the filament's curve instead of cutting straight across it.

**Clamp tail.** The rear appendage ends in two curved gripping surfaces lined with friction ridges. A contractile band closes them around a hair; an elastic hinge reopens the clamp when tension eases. The palp filament passing through this clamp belongs to Hydrosaltator and is the feeding substrate, not part of the mite's anatomy.

**Sensory and digestive systems.** Short feelers near the mouth register movement and surface oils. A compact digestive sac occupies the central body, with storage droplets along its sides and an outlet toward the rear. Brood tissue lies beneath the protected dorsal shield, away from the exposed combing edge.

## Relationships in the wider food web

Away from the tick, the Moustache Mite supplements its diet by cropping living *Mycocladus sulfuris* filament tips. Its comb teeth pinch the soft growing ends, and the digestive sac processes the collected living material. This is direct predation, distinct from its persistent parasitic grazing on the tick’s palps.
