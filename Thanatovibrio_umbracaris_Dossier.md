# Scientific Dossier: *Thanatovibrio umbracaris*

## Profile

- **Scientific Name:** *Thanatovibrio umbracaris*
- **Common Names:** Graveglass Microbe, Black Choir
- **Size:** 2.4 micrometers (active filament); colonies can span several meters
- **Colony Form:** Threadlike mats with a brittle, obsidian-colored silica sheath
- **Cellular Structure:** Distributed colony intelligence; no permanent central organelle

## Habitat and Behavior

- **Habitat:** Abandoned hydrothermal-vent tunnels and lightless brine pockets
- **Active Range:** 4-18 degrees C
- **Dormancy Trigger:** Above 22 degrees C or sudden bright light
- **Lethal Limit:** Sustained exposure above 41 degrees C
- **Movement:** Follows vibration through rock and metal rather than swimming
- **Signature:** A low, harmonic clicking that appears to answer nearby sounds

The organism feeds on dissolved minerals and the trace electrical gradients around old equipment. It builds a glassy sheath around anything that remains still long enough, gradually turning a habitat into a maze of black, ringing filaments.

## Threat Profile

1. **Echo mimicry:** Colonies reproduce nearby sounds with a delay, making empty tunnels seem occupied.
2. **Silica bloom:** Contact with a mature mat causes rapid mineral crusting of exposed surfaces.
3. **Colony memory:** Separated fragments retain the same rhythmic pattern and slowly grow toward one another.
4. **Light aversion:** Bright light drives the colony into cracks, where it can remain dormant for decades.

## Containment Lore

In-universe surveys mark any clicking tunnel as **BLACK CHOIR - DO NOT ANSWER**. The only reliable field sign is a fresh line of dark glass crossing an otherwise clean surface.

*The expanded behavior and anatomy below are speculative worldbuilding for this fictional organism.*

## Detailed behavior

Graveglass colonies extend along surfaces carrying repeated vibration, concentrating growth at contact points in rock or abandoned equipment. A disturbance passes through the connected mat as a delayed sequence of contractions and clicks. Repeated inputs reinforce favored pathways, providing the setting's distributed colony memory without a permanent command center.

Feeding draws on dissolved minerals and weak electrical gradients while new silica accumulates around stationary surfaces. Older branches become rigid conduits and younger tips explore adjoining cracks. Separated fragments preserve portions of the rhythm and extend toward compatible vibrations. Bright light or temperatures above 22 degrees C halt exposed growth and favor retreat into protected crevices; activity belongs to the established 4-18 degrees C range. Sustained heat above 41 degrees C exceeds the organism's stated tolerance.

## Detailed anatomy

**Living filament.** The active filament is recorded at 2.4 micrometers and forms part of a much larger threadlike mat. A narrow living strand runs inside each sheath, with membrane-bounded cellular compartments arranged along its length. There is no permanent central organelle governing the colony; local changes propagate through connected junctions.

**Obsidian sheath.** Successive silica deposits build a dark, brittle sleeve around the living strand. Thin growth rings mark earlier deposition fronts. Small discontinuities permit exchange with the surrounding water, while thicker mature sections provide the rigid surfaces responsible for the colony's glassy appearance and fictional resonance.

**Junctions and sensory contacts.** Branch points connect several strands through softer living bridges. Contact patches pressed against stone or metal register mechanical strain and local electrical differences. The network's many attachment points let a disturbance enter at one location and influence growth elsewhere without swimming through open water.

**Growth fronts and reserves.** Young tips have thinner, more flexible coverings and secretion zones that lay down fresh silica behind the advancing edge. Minute reserve inclusions support repair during interruptions in feeding. Fracture-prone necks allow sections to separate, and exposed living ends seal before extending a new sheath.

**Dormant architecture.** During dormancy, activity contracts into protected portions of the network, leaving empty or inactive glass branches outside. These residual sleeves can retain a colony's outline long after the living growth front has withdrawn, so visible black glass alone does not establish current activity.

## Relationships in the wider food web

Black Choir Virus can infect compatible living Graveglass compartments in old, cold mineral films. Empty silica sleeves remain after local cell loss. Currents transport shed silica fragments to Ironback Mason terraces; this exchange involves detached mineral material and does not move heat-sensitive living Graveglass into hot channels.
