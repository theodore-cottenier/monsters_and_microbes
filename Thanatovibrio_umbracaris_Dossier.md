## Profile

- **Scientific Name:** *Thanatovibrio umbracaris*
- **Common Names:** Graveglass Microbe, Black Choir

- **Colony Form:** Threadlike mats with a brittle, obsidian-colored silica sheath
- **Cellular Structure:** Distributed colony intelligence; no permanent central organelle

## Habitat and Behavior

- **Habitat:** Abandoned hydrothermal-vent tunnels and lightless brine pockets
- **Active Range:** 4-18 degrees C
- **Dormancy Trigger:** Above 22 degrees C or sudden bright light
- **Lethal Limit:** Sustained exposure above 41 degrees C
- **Movement:** Follows vibration through rock and metal rather than swimming
- **Signature:** A low, harmonic clicking that appears to answer nearby sounds

The organism feeds on dissolved minerals and the trace electrical gradients around old equipment. It builds a glassy sheath around anything that remains still long enough, gradually turning a habitat into a maze of black, ringing filaments.

## Threat Profile (fictional)

1. **Echo mimicry:** Colonies reproduce nearby sounds with a delay, making empty tunnels seem occupied.
2. **Silica bloom:** Contact with a mature mat causes rapid mineral crusting of exposed surfaces.
3. **Colony memory:** Separated fragments retain the same rhythmic pattern and slowly grow toward one another.
4. **Light aversion:** Bright light drives the colony into cracks, where it can remain dormant for decades.
